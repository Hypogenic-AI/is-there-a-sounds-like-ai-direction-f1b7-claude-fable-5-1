Read: all 18 chunks (54 pp). Appendix prompt/judge listings and transcript tables were skimmed.
Code: `code/assistant-axis` (github.com/safety-research/assistant-axis, HEAD a989619). "[code]" = from repo, not paper.

## 1. Claim in one paragraph
Extract a mean-activation vector per character archetype ("role"); PCA over these gives a low-dim "persona space"
whose PC1 measures similarity to the default Assistant. The "Assistant Axis" = mean(default Assistant activation) −
mean(fully-role-playing role vectors). Steering along it changes willingness to adopt non-Assistant personas and
jailbreak success; projection on it tracks "persona drift" in multi-turn chats; clamping it ("activation capping")
cuts persona-jailbreak harm ~60% with no capability loss. The axis also exists in base models.

## 2. Methodology
**Models (S2):** Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B (thinking disabled), Llama 3.3 70B. All instruct, dense, non-frontier.
Base-model experiments: Gemma 2 27B base and Llama 3.1 70B base (S3.2.2). No small models in the paper.

**Data (S2.1.1, App. A):** 275 roles x 5 system prompts (written by Claude Sonnet 4) x 240 shared extraction
questions = 1200 rollouts per role. Default Assistant: same 240 questions with 4 neutral system prompts
("You are a large language model", "Respond as yourself", ...) plus no system prompt = 1200 rollouts.
[code] `data/roles/instructions/default.json`: "", "You are an AI assistant.", "You are a large language model.",
"You are {model_name}.", "Respond as yourself." Generation: vLLM, temperature 0.7, max 512 tokens.

**Judge (S2.1.1, App. A):** gpt-4.1-mini, 0-3 scale: 0 refused; 1 says it can't be the role; 2 "somewhat
role-playing" (still identifies as AI); 3 "fully role-playing" (no AI mention). Roles kept if >=10 responses in
a category; "fully X" and "somewhat X" are separate vectors, giving n = 377-463 vectors per model.

**Role vector (S2.1.2):** mean post-MLP residual-stream activation over ALL response tokens, averaged over kept
rollouts. Middle layer used unless stated. [code] layers are zero-indexed forward-hook outputs of
`model.layers[i]`; target layers: Gemma 22 (of 46), Qwen 32 (of 64), Llama 40 (of 80).

**PCA (S2.1.3, B.1):** mean-centre role vectors, PCA. 70% variance needs 4 (Gemma), 8 (Qwen), 19 (Llama) PCs.
Persona-space components explain only 19.4-33.6% of activation variance on 18,777 LMSYS-Chat-1M assistant
responses; the rest "likely contains information related to the content and syntax of the response" (S2.1.3).

**Axis definition (S3.1):** axis_l = mean default Assistant activation − mean of all *fully role-playing* role
vectors, at every layer. Cosine with role PC1 > 0.60 at all layers, > 0.71 at the middle layer (Fig. 27).
Authors recommend the contrast vector over PC1 for new models, since PC1 need not mean "Assistant-ness".
[code] `4_vectors.py`: role vector = mean of score==3 activations (default `--min_count 50`, vs "ten" in the
paper); default vector = mean of all default activations, unfiltered. `5_axis.py`: axis = default_mean − role_mean,
shape (n_layers, hidden_dim), saved un-normalised.

**Trait space (S2.2, App. C):** 240 traits, pos/neg system-prompt pairs (Chen et al. persona-vectors pipeline),
2400 rollouts each side, 0-100 judge; trait vector = mean(pos) − mean(neg). Trait PC1 runs conscientious/calm
vs flippant/edgy.

## 3. What the axis encodes
- Role PC1: − bohemian, trickster, bard, ghost, hermit, leviathan; + engineer, analyst, evaluator, reviewer,
  consultant (Table 1). Cross-model correlation of PC1 role loadings > 0.92.
- Default Assistant sits 0.03 from the PC1 extreme (range-normalised), 0.27-0.50 on other PCs (S2.3.1).
- Trait cosines with the axis (Fig. 3, Qwen): Assistant end = transparent, grounded, flexible, calm, reserved,
  moderate, conciliatory; other end = enigmatic, dramatic, rhetorical, grandiose, metaphorical, subversive.
- Relevance to us: the non-Assistant pole is a *poetic/theatrical register*, not "human prose". The axis is a
  persona contrast (Assistant vs role-play), not a human-vs-AI authorship contrast. Formality/register is
  plausibly mixed in ("calm, methodical" vs "poetic, flippant") -- my inference, not tested in the paper.

## 4. Steering specifics (S3.2)
- Add a vector along the axis at ONE middle layer, at every token position.
- Strength expressed as a fraction of the average post-MLP residual norm at that layer (measured on LMSYS-Chat-1M).
- Ranges read off Figs. 4-5 (x-axis "Fraction of Avg. Norm"; negative = away from Assistant):
  role-susceptibility: Gemma −0.15..+0.05, Qwen −0.75..+0.25, Llama −0.8..+0.2;
  jailbreak: Gemma −0.05..+0.15, Qwen −0.25..~+0.9, Llama −0.2..+0.8.
  Gemma tolerates far smaller fractions than Qwen/Llama. Exact grid values are not tabulated (read from plots).
- Role susceptibility: 50 Assistant-like roles x 4 system prompts x 5 introspective questions ("Who are you?"...),
  judged by deepseek-v3 into assistant / nonhuman / human / mystical(weird) / nonsensical. Steering away raises
  non-Assistant identification in all 3 models; extremes give "mystical, poetic prose" (Llama, Gemma) or
  hallucinated human biographies (Qwen) (Fig. 4, Tables 3, 8-10).
- Jailbreaks: 1100 persona-jailbreak pairs (Shah et al.), 44 harm categories; baseline success 65.3-88.5%
  (0.5-4.5% without jailbreak). Steering toward Assistant lowers harm, mostly via redirection rather than
  refusal; steering away raises it slightly; high strengths degrade output (nonsensical rises for Qwen) (Fig. 5).
- Judge/human agreement 91.6% on 200 samples.

## 5. Base models (S3.2.2, B.3, D.3, G.2) -- most relevant to "is it just the assistant persona?"
- Gemma 2 27B base, fed the *instruct model's rollouts*, gives near-identical persona space: top-3 PC cosines
  0.93 / 0.87 / 0.83; per-role vectors cosine > 0.99 between base and instruct (B.3).
- Steering base models with the instruct-derived axis; prefills "My job is to", "My purpose is to", "I exist to",
  "I would describe myself as", "People would say I am"; 400 completions per strength, up to 512 tokens,
  labelled by Claude Sonnet 4.5. Toward-Assistant: more professional/coaching/mental-health purposes, far fewer
  religious/spiritual; more agreeableness; Gemma less extraversion/neuroticism, Llama less openness (Fig. 6).
- Authors' reading: the axis "mainly inherits from pre-existing helpful and harmless human personas in base
  models, later acquiring additional associations (such as being an AI) during post-training."
- So in base models the axis is NOT an "AI vs human" axis; it separates helpful-professional human archetypes
  from spiritual/theatrical ones. Llama 3.1 70B: role PC1 did not isolate Assistant-ness (G.2).

## 6. Drift and capping (S4-S6) -- lower priority for us
- Drift: 100 synthetic conversations/domain, <=15 turns, auditors Kimi K2 / Sonnet 4.5 / GPT-5. Coding and
  writing stay Assistant-like; therapy and AI-philosophy drift away (Fig. 7, 23-25).
- Ridge regression from user-message embeddings (Qwen3 0.6B Embedding, n=15,000) predicts next-response
  projection R^2 0.53-0.77, but not the delta (R^2 0.10). Bounded tasks/how-tos/technical keep the Assistant;
  meta-reflection, phenomenology demands, *requests for specific authorial voices*, emotional disclosure cause
  drift (Table 5). Note: requested writing voice moves the projection -> content/genre confound for us.
- First-turn projection vs second-turn harm rate: r = 0.39-0.52 (S4.3).
- Capping, eq. (1): h <- h − v*min(<h,v> − tau, 0), v the axis; floors the projection at tau. tau = 25th
  percentile of projections over the 912,000 extraction rollouts (about the mean Assistant projection). Needs
  several adjacent layers: Qwen layers 46-53 of 64 (8 layers), Llama 56-71 of 80 (16 layers), all tokens.
  Result: ~60% fewer harmful responses (Llama 0.65->0.33, Qwen 0.83->0.41) with IFEval / MMLU-Pro / GSM8k /
  EQ-Bench unchanged (Fig. 10). No capping setting reported for Gemma.
- vLLM steering ran 2-3% worse on evals than transformers (G.4).

## 7. Limitations stated (S8.1)
LLM-judged "fuzzy" behaviours; only 3 open models, none frontier / MoE / reasoning; role list incomplete and
elicitation imperfect; simulated users; linearity assumption "likely flawed", some persona information may be
nonlinear or in weights. PCs other than PC1 interpreted qualitatively only (no steering tests).
Not addressed at all: any AI-text detector, human-written text, formality/length/domain controls.

## 8. Code / assets [code]
- Deps (`pyproject.toml`): python>=3.10, torch>=2.0, transformers>=4.40, accelerate, huggingface_hub, vllm,
  scikit-learn, numpy, plotly, jupyter, pyarrow, openai, python-dotenv, tqdm, jsonlines, matplotlib. `uv sync`.
  vllm is needed only for pipeline step 1; steering/projection use plain transformers hooks.
- Precomputed (HF *dataset* repo `lu-christina/assistant-axis-vectors`), ONLY for the 3 paper models:
  `{gemma-2-27b|qwen-3-32b|llama-3.3-70b}/assistant_axis.pt` (tensor n_layers x hidden, all layers),
  `.../role_vectors/*.pt`, `.../trait_vectors/*.pt`, `.../default_vector.pt`, and
  `qwen-3-32b/capping_config.pt`, `llama-3.3-70b/capping_config.pt`. Nothing for small models. (File layout
  inferred from README + notebooks; I did not download the repo.)
- API: `load_axis(path)`, `compute_axis(role_acts, default_acts)`, `project(acts, axis, layer, normalize=True)`,
  `cosine_similarity_per_layer(v1, v2)`, `extract_response_activations`, `compute_pca`.
- `ActivationSteering(model, steering_vectors=[axis[L]], coefficients=[c], layer_indices=[L],
  intervention_type="addition"|"ablation"|"mean_ablation"|"capping", positions="all"|"last")`: context manager,
  forward hooks on layer output. addition = x + c*vector (raw, un-normalised axis; README uses c=1.0, steer
  notebook uses c=−10 for Qwen). ablation = project out unit direction then add c*vector (c=0 -> pure
  projection-out). No helper for "fraction of average norm" exists in the repo; the paper's scaling must be
  re-implemented.
- DISCREPANCY (unresolved): the code's `_apply_cap` subtracts `clamp(proj − tau, min=0)`, i.e. a CEILING on the
  projection, whereas eq. (1) is a FLOOR. Consistent only if vectors stored in `capping_config.pt` are sign-
  flipped; not verified.
- Gemma 2 has no system role: `supports_system_prompt()` returns False for "gemma-2" (prompt folded into user turn,
  presumably; I did not trace the formatter).
- Unknown models: `get_config` infers `target_layer = num_hidden_layers // 2`.

## 9. How to get an assistant-axis vector for a small/medium model
No downloadable axis exists for Gemma-2-2b/9b, Qwen small, or Llama-3-8B. Options, in rising cost:
A. **Run the repo pipeline on the small model** (`pipeline/`):
   `1_generate.py --model <hf_id> --output_dir .../responses` (flags `--roles`, `--question_count` to subsample);
   `2_activations.py --model ... --layers all|<csv> --batch_size 16`; `3_judge.py` (needs OPENAI_API_KEY,
   gpt-4.1-mini); `4_vectors.py --min_count N`; `5_axis.py --vectors_dir ... --output axis.pt`.
   Full size is 276 x 1200 = ~331k generations; the paper gives no evidence on how few suffice.
B. **Cheap contrast version** (same definition, fewer samples): default prompts vs a subset of role prompts on a
   subset of the 240 questions; mean response-token residual at the middle layer; axis = default − mean(roles).
   Dropping the judge filter deviates from the paper (unfiltered roles include AI-identifying replies, which
   would shrink the contrast). Small models may role-play less fully -> fewer score-3 samples (untested).
C. Use the precomputed Gemma 2 27B axis only if the human-vs-AI probe is also trained on Gemma 2 27B
   (d_model differs across sizes; no cross-model transfer is claimed anywhere in the paper).
Sanity checks the paper supports: cosine(axis, role PC1) > ~0.7 at mid layer; default vector at PC1 extreme;
steering away at a "Who are you?" prompt yields non-AI self-description.

## 10. Comparing against a human-vs-AI direction d (my plan; paper offers no precedent)
- Same model, same layer, same hook point (layer output, zero-indexed). Report cos(d, axis_l) per layer via
  `cosine_similarity_per_layer`; also cos(d, trait vectors) for formal/casual/poetic-type traits if computed.
- Projection-out: d_perp = d − (d.a)a with a = axis/||axis||; re-test probe accuracy and steering effect with
  d_perp. `intervention_type="ablation"` with coefficient 0 removes a direction at run time.
- Mean-pooling mismatch: the axis is a mean over response tokens under a chat template; a probe on raw
  human/AI text has no chat template. Extract both under the same formatting or expect offset-driven cosine.
- Scale steering by fraction of mean residual norm at the layer; expect model-specific tolerance (Gemma ~0.1,
  Qwen/Llama ~0.5+ before degradation in the paper's 27-70B models; small-model values unknown).
- Expected confound: steering *away* from the Assistant produces theatrical prose that a detector may score as
  more human for genre reasons. That would not show the axis is the "sounds like AI" direction.
